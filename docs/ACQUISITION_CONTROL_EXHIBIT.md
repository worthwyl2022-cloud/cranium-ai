## 1. Purpose and disclosure

The acquiring party acknowledges that Cranium includes a constitutional governance layer, Prime Directives, a monthly diagnostics and evolution cycle, an owner control plane, and cryptographic verification mechanisms. These are intentional product and safety features, not undocumented restrictions, hidden access mechanisms, or defects.

The acquiring party acknowledges that these mechanisms are part of the Cranium architecture and are material to its identity, provenance, governance, safety, continuity, and operation.

## 2. Protected constitutional artifacts

The protected artifacts include the Cranium Constitution, Prime Directives, constitutional hash and verification process, capability boundaries, owner-control specification, approval receipts, audit records, rollback lineage, and the rules governing self-improvement and self-healing.

No protected artifact may be removed, weakened, bypassed, replaced, or materially changed during the protection period except through the approval process described below.

## 5. Monthly diagnostics and evolution

At least once every 30 days, the system may run a documented maintenance cycle that verifies constitutional integrity, executes diagnostics, runs regression tests, builds the application, evaluates proposed improvements, and generates an auditable report.

The system may improve itself only within an explicitly allowlisted scope, such as prompt wording, pronunciation mappings, UI polish, regression tests, and non-security documentation. Proposed changes must be evaluated, fingerprinted, versioned, attributable, and reversible.

Changes affecting identity, mission, Prime Directives, Constitution, governance, authority, safety boundaries, autonomy, self-modification controls, permissions, credentials, billing, data rights, external publishing, or acquisition obligations are major changes and require the protected approver’s signed authorization before implementation, merge, deployment, or representation as accepted.

## 6. Fail-closed and recovery behavior

If a protected artifact, expected hash, approval record, or verification mechanism is altered without valid authorization, the system shall enter read-only safe mode for change-related operations, preserve tamper evidence, generate an alert, and refuse the proposed change. The system shall not erase itself, execute destructive code, corrupt data, or destroy infrastructure.

## 7. Notice and material change

The acquiring party shall notify the protected approver before requesting, authorizing, or implementing any major change. Notification shall include the proposed change, rationale, affected components, risk assessment, test results, rollback plan, and expected effect on the Constitution, Prime Directives, governance, or owner-control system.

## 8. Continuity and audit

The acquiring party shall preserve release lineage, constitutional hashes, approval receipts, maintenance reports, tamper alerts, and owner-control audit records for the protection period and shall provide the protected approver reasonable access to verify compliance.

## 9. Expiration

The protection period expires five years after the acquisition closing date unless extended by a written agreement signed by the protected approver and the acquiring party. Expiration does not retroactively invalidate records, approvals, or audit evidence created during the protection period.
